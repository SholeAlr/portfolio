Most AI prompts are designed to get an answer.

I wanted to build one that gets me a **question instead**.

Not a random conspiracy theory.  
Not a "10 mysteries that will blow your mind" listicle.  
Not something that is only mysterious because nobody bothered to Google it.

I wanted something different:

> Give me something we genuinely don't know.

Something that has been investigated by serious researchers, historians, scientists, linguists, mathematicians, or other experts — but where the evidence still doesn't give us a definitive answer.

And then...

**Tell me the story.**

## The idea

The prompt asks AI to act less like a search engine and more like an investigator.

It can look for mysteries anywhere:

- 🔬 Science
- 🏛️ History
- 🎨 Art
- 🎵 Music
- 📚 Literature
- 🗣️ Linguistics
- 🧠 Psychology
- 🧮 Mathematics
- 🏺 Anthropology
- 💻 Technology
- 🧠 Philosophy
- ...or basically anywhere humans have managed to say _"huh, that's weird."_

The important part is that the mystery has to be **real**.

There should be evidence.

People should have actually investigated it.

There should be multiple serious explanations.

And most importantly, there shouldn't already be a definitive answer.

## The prompt

Copy this into your favorite AI:

```text
Find me a genuinely unresolved intellectual mystery.

The mystery can come from ANY serious field, including science, history, archaeology, anthropology, linguistics, literature, art, music, philosophy, mathematics, psychology, technology, or other academic disciplines.

Do NOT limit yourself to famous mysteries. I am more interested in fascinating, well-evidenced mysteries that most people have never heard of.

The mystery MUST:

- Have credible evidence, documents, objects, observations, experiments, or other reliable sources behind it.
- Have been seriously investigated by researchers, scholars, experts, or other credible investigators.
- Have at least two plausible competing explanations or interpretations.
- Still lack a widely accepted definitive explanation.
- Be interesting enough to tell as a compelling story.
- NOT be primarily based on rumors, folklore, anecdotes, conspiracy theories, paranormal claims, or pseudoscience.
- NOT be something that has a simple, well-established answer that is merely obscure to the general public.

Once you find a suitable mystery, tell me the story rather than simply giving me a factual summary.

Structure the investigation like a detective story:

1. Start with a strong hook.

Introduce the mystery in a way that immediately makes me curious. Do not begin with a dry definition or encyclopedia-style introduction.

2. Tell me what happened.

Give me enough historical, scientific, cultural, or contextual background to understand why this mystery exists.

3. Introduce the first explanation.

Explain what people originally believed was happening and why that explanation seemed convincing.

4. Show what didn't fit.

What evidence, discovery, contradiction, experiment, document, or observation made people question the original explanation?

5. Follow the investigation.

Explain how researchers tried to solve the mystery and what they discovered along the way.

6. Introduce the competing explanations.

For each major hypothesis, explain:

- What it claims.
- Why it is plausible.
- What evidence supports it.
- What evidence contradicts it.
- Which researchers or experts support it, if relevant.

7. Separate the evidence from the speculation.

Clearly distinguish between:

- What we KNOW.
- What researchers STRONGLY SUSPECT.
- What is DISPUTED.
- What is merely SPECULATIVE.

Do not present speculation as fact.

8. Explain which theories have been rejected or weakened.

If an explanation was once popular but later disproved, contradicted, or significantly weakened by new evidence, explain what happened and why.

9. Tell me where we are today.

What is the current scholarly or scientific understanding of the mystery?

Do experts generally favor one explanation, or is the evidence still genuinely divided?

10. Rank the hypotheses.

Rank the major explanations from strongest to weakest based on the available evidence.

Explain why you ranked them that way.

Do NOT rank them based on how exciting or mysterious they sound.

11. End with "What Would Solve It?"

Explain exactly what kind of new evidence, experiment, discovery, document, technology, or observation could finally resolve the mystery.

The goal is not to make the mystery sound more mysterious than it really is.

The goal is to show how far human knowledge has taken us — and exactly where it stops.

Use high-quality sources whenever possible, prioritizing:

- Peer-reviewed research
- Academic books and journals
- Universities and research institutions
- Museums, libraries, and archives
- Government or scientific organizations
- Primary historical sources
- Reputable investigative journalism

Include sources and links for the important claims you make.

Avoid sensationalism. If the mystery has a partial explanation, say so.

I want to finish the story thinking:

"We actually know a LOT about this...

...but somehow, we still don't know THAT."
```
